## Verse 1
- Bars: 1-8
- Key: D minor. Meter 6/8 at dotted quarter = 60 (eighth = 180).
- Chords: Dm | Dm/C# | Dm/C | G/B | Gm/Bb | A7 | Dm/A | A7. Bass falls D-C#-C-B-Bb-A over bars 1-6, then sits on A.
- Contrast levers: rocking 3+3 lilt; low register C#4 to Bb4 (B natural on the G/B bar); stepwise lines; lines 1, 3 and 4 open after an eighth rest; a fado turn (A-Bb-A) on "não" in bar 6. Rhyme open: porta / horas is assonance on o-a; enfermeira / abre does not rhyme.
- Story: you count the hours. The clock names you, "enfermeira" (bar 4), so the cold reader knows who you are. Three o'clock, the sealed window, and the patient: the morphine drips his hours away (bars 7-8). Comfort care only. He will not be saved.

## Chorus
- Bars: 9-14
- Key: D minor. Meter 4/4 at quarter = 90 (same eighth = 180; the pulse regroups from 3+3 to 2+2+2+2).
- Chords: Dm | Dm/C | Bb | A7 | Dm | A7. Andalusian descent D-C-Bb-A.
- Contrast levers: straight 4/4; register lifts a fourth, A4 to D5; the hook starts on the downbeat (the verses start after a rest); the hook rhythm limps long-short (3+1, 3+1, 3+1); the voice stops for half a bar after each title line, and the guitar plays the hook cell back (bars 10 and 14). Rhyme closes: mãe / vem is a perfect rhyme in European Portuguese.
- Story: his request, in his words. He names the song: "fado do sal da mãe", his mother's salt fado. The tram is named as Lisbon's 28 (bar 12). The title sits on the first and last line.

## Verse 2
- Bars: 15-22
- Key, meter, chords: as Verse 1.
- Contrast levers: as Verse 1, plus line 1 opens with "Ele" on two eighths (bar 15), so he is plainly the one lending, and ends on a held stressed note ("mãe", bar 16), and "e não sabes a letra" is cut short (bar 22). It leaves three eighths of silence for the guitar before the last chorus.
- New information: he lends you his mother's cardigan, it smells of the Alcochete salt pans, his grip is failing, and you do not know the words of her song.

## Chorus (final)
- Bars: 23-29
- Key: D minor. 4/4 at quarter = 90.
- Chords: Dm | Dm/C | Bb | A7 | Dm | Dm/C# | Dm/C. The last three bars bring the verse's chromatic bass D-C#-C back under the held note.
- Contrast levers: the first line turns from his order to your act ("Canta" becomes "Cantas", one extra eighth in bar 23); line 2 answers bar 22: you sing without the words, only saudade, with "sem" as a pickup at the end of bar 24 (the guitar gap there shrinks to three eighths) and "DA" held a dotted quarter on D5; line 3 names the bell, "o sino do elétrico", so five o'clock arrives in bar 26; the rhyme breaks; the hook's leap widens from a fourth (A4-D5) to a minor sixth (A4-F5) on "até", held ten eighths across bars 27-28. F5 appears nowhere else.
- Emotional move: Verse 1 counts the hours to dawn. The bell rings five in bar 26, and you keep singing after it: the held "até" (until) in bars 27-28 is sung past five, as if dawn could be held off.
