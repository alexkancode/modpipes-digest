Rain on the shelter roof like pennies, you read the timetable, I read you. Twenty years, your collar's up, the left side only, like at school.

You turn a ring I've never seen. My last to you is one line long: Dear Ruth, it's raining here, then nothing. And neither of us mentions Tom.

Production brief

Genre: indie folk

  • Tempo: 96 BPM, 4/4, steady. No tempo change.
  • Key: D major. Guitar may play in C shapes with capo 2.
  • Length: 36 bars, about 90 seconds. One bar of guitar may lead in. End on the last "well", held, and let the chord ring out.

Arrangement

  • Verse 1: one fingerpicked steel-string acoustic guitar and voice. Thumb on the bass notes, a light pattern on the top strings. Nothing else.
  • Chorus 1: the guitar moves to soft strumming with accents on 2 and 4. Upright bass enters on the chord roots. Brushes on a snare, very quiet. A second voice sings a third above on "you look well" only.
  • Verse 2: pull back. Bass drops out. Guitar picks as in verse 1, a little sparser. Brushes stay on as a faint, even patter, the rain on the roof.
  • Bridge: voice, guitar and the faint brush patter. The guitar holds each chord as a single strum and lets it ring. The C chord in bar 26, under "waits. Neither moves", is the darkest moment; leave space after "moves".
  • Seam into the final chorus: the brush patter stops dead at the end of bar 28, as if the rain stopped. One beat of near silence, then the last chorus.
  • Final chorus: subtract, do not swell. Fingerpicked guitar and voice, bass back in softly. The second voice joins only on the last "you look well". No drums.

Instruments

Steel-string acoustic guitar, upright bass, brushed snare, a second voice. No electric guitar, no strings, no pads, no stomp or clap.

Vocal direction

One lead voice, low and close to the mic, plain and unforced, as if talking to someone a metre away. Sing the verses almost spoken. Let "well" open out and sustain without vibrato until the end of the note. Do not lean on "Tom"; sing it flat and let the silence after it do the work. The harmony voice is soft and slightly behind.

Mood

Polite on top, full underneath. Two people being careful with each other. Warm, not sad; the last chorus should feel like a choice to stay.

Sound and space

Close-miked, small room. Leave pick noise and breaths in. Narrow stereo image. A short room reverb, about two seconds on the held notes. No rain sound effects; the brushes and the picked guitar carry the patter.

Reference points in plain words

A quiet late-night acoustic recording made in one room. A fingerpicked folk ballad with one voice and one guitar, where the band comes in only for the chorus and leaves again.

About
Two people who were once close meet by chance at a bus shelter after twenty years, trade polite small talk, and leave without mentioning what went unsaid, including a letter that got no reply and someone named Tom.
Hook line
You look well, and you say the same of me
Reader notes
The repeated "You look well" works as a hook because the politeness covers a lot of unspoken feeling, and details like the one-sided collar, the unseen ring, the one-line letter to Ruth and the unsent stamp land well; the one thing that doesn't is the bridge, which is a bit cryptic about who the stamp is for and what 'waits' means, and the last chorus changes 'then both of us say' to 'then I wave on', a shift a first-time listener might miss.
Match reason
The reading keeps the theme's core elements (two old friends, a chance meeting at a bus stop after twenty years, and an unspoken silence about why they stopped), and its added details, the unanswered letter and Tom, are plausible specifics that don't contradict the theme.
